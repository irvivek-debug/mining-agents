# Work Order Prioritisation Standard

**Document reference:** MAINT-WOP-002  
**Authorising role:** General Manager — Fixed Plant and Infrastructure  
**Revision:** 5  
**Effective date:** 2024-06-15  
**Review cycle:** 12 months or following any unplanned equipment failure attributable to a prioritisation decision, whichever is sooner  

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

1.1 This standard establishes the priority categories for maintenance work orders, the conditions under which a work order must be reviewed for escalation, the lead-time thresholds governing parts procurement relative to scheduling, and the prohibition on priority manipulation.

1.2 It applies to all corrective, preventive and predictive maintenance work orders raised against fixed plant, mobile equipment and infrastructure assets on site. It applies to the Maintenance Planner, Scheduler, Supervisor and any personnel with system access to create or modify work orders in the Computerised Maintenance Management System (CMMS).

---

## 2. Priority Categories

2.1 Work orders are classified at creation under one of four priority levels:

**Priority 1 — Immediate.** Asset failure or imminent risk of failure presents an active safety hazard or has caused unplanned cessation of a primary production process. Response required within 4 hours of work order creation; work must commence within 8 hours.

**Priority 2 — Urgent.** Degraded asset condition that, if unaddressed, will result in a Priority 1 condition within 72 hours, or a regulatory compliance obligation with a fixed due date within 14 days. Work must commence within 48 hours.

**Priority 3 — Routine.** Scheduled preventive or predictive maintenance, or corrective work where the asset remains operational within design limits. Work must be scheduled within the next 4-week planning cycle.

**Priority 4 — Deferred.** Work identified as necessary but with no near-term operational or compliance consequence. Work must be reviewed at least every 90 days to confirm deferral remains appropriate.

2.2 The initial priority classification is made by the Maintenance Planner in consultation with the relevant Area Supervisor. Changes to priority after initial classification require documented justification and the countersignature of the Maintenance Superintendent.

---

## 3. Age-Based Review for Escalation

3.1 Any open work order that has not progressed to the "Scheduled" status in the CMMS within the following periods must be reviewed by the Maintenance Planner and the responsible Supervisor to determine whether escalation to a higher priority is warranted:

a. Priority 1: 4 hours from creation.  
b. Priority 2: 48 hours from creation.  
c. Priority 3: 30 days from creation.  
d. Priority 4: 90 days from creation.

3.2 The review must be documented in the CMMS work order history field. If the Planner and Supervisor determine that escalation is not warranted, they must record the basis for that determination and countersign the record.

3.3 Any Priority 3 work order that remains open and unscheduled for more than 60 days is automatically flagged in the CMMS weekly review report for the Maintenance Superintendent's attention.

3.4 Any Priority 4 work order that remains open for more than 180 days without documented re-confirmation of deferred status is automatically escalated to Priority 3 in the CMMS and must enter the scheduling pipeline within the next planning cycle.

---

## 4. Parts Lead-Time and Procurement Threshold

4.1 Before a work order is confirmed as schedulable, the Maintenance Planner must verify that all required parts and materials are available in stores, on order with a confirmed delivery date, or obtainable within the scheduling window.

4.2 Where one or more required parts carries a procurement lead time exceeding 14 days, the parts order must be raised before the work order is confirmed as scheduled. The work order must not be moved to "Scheduled" status until the purchase order number is recorded in the CMMS work order against each affected part.

4.3 Where the procurement lead time exceeds 45 days, the Maintenance Planner must notify the Maintenance Superintendent and the relevant Area Superintendent in writing within 2 business days of identifying the lead time. The notification must include an assessment of operational risk during the procurement period and any interim control measures proposed.

4.4 Emergency procurement outside the standard approval chain may be authorised by the Maintenance Superintendent for Priority 1 and Priority 2 work orders. All emergency procurements must be ratified through the standard purchase order process within 5 business days of the emergency authorisation.

---

## 5. Prohibition on Priority Manipulation

5.1 A work order's priority level must reflect the genuine operational and safety condition of the asset. Priority may not be raised for the purpose of advancing a parts procurement request, obtaining a purchase order number, or meeting a procurement approval threshold.

5.2 The following actions constitute priority manipulation and must be reported to the Maintenance Superintendent and the site Integrity and Compliance function:

a. Classifying a Priority 3 or Priority 4 work order as Priority 1 or Priority 2 in the absence of the conditions described in clause 2.1.  
b. Amending a priority classification without the countersignature required under clause 2.2 for the sole purpose of triggering emergency procurement under clause 4.4.  
c. Splitting a single scope of work across multiple work orders to avoid the lead-time notification obligation in clause 4.3.

5.3 Priority manipulation findings identified through audit or system review are treated as a breach of this standard and managed under the site Disciplinary Policy.

5.4 The Maintenance Planner is not authorised to approve priority upgrades on work orders where they also hold the role of requestor. In such cases, the Maintenance Superintendent must review and approve the classification.

---

## 6. Records and Reporting

6.1 The CMMS must be the system of record for all work order priority classifications, review outcomes, escalations and procurement lead-time notifications. Manual records that duplicate CMMS data are not authoritative.

6.2 The Maintenance Superintendent must review the age-based escalation report weekly and sign off that all work orders meeting the review thresholds in clause 3 have been actioned.

6.3 A monthly summary of work order ageing, escalation counts and outstanding Priority 4 deferrals must be provided to the General Manager — Fixed Plant and Infrastructure.

6.4 All work order records must be retained in the CMMS for a minimum of 7 years from the date of closure.
