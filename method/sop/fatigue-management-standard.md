# Fatigue Management Standard

**Document reference:** OPS-FMS-001  
**Authorising role:** General Manager — Operations  
**Revision:** 3  
**Effective date:** 2024-03-01  
**Review cycle:** 24 months or following any fatigue-related incident, whichever is sooner  

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

1.1 This standard establishes the thresholds, monitoring obligations and response protocols that govern fatigue risk for all personnel performing safety-critical tasks at the operation. It applies to site-based employees, contractors and any visitor who performs work involving mobile equipment operation, confined-space entry, work at height, or process control.

1.2 This standard does not apply to office-based administrative tasks that carry no direct interface with physical hazards.

---

## 2. Definitions

2.1 **Biometric alert** — a flag generated by an approved operator-monitoring device when a measured indicator (eyelid closure rate, steering micro-correction frequency, reaction-time deviation, or equivalent) exceeds the device manufacturer's fatigue-detection threshold for a sustained period.

2.2 **Sleep deficit** — the shortfall between the minimum restorative sleep target (defined in clause 4.1) and the sleep actually obtained by a worker in the reference period.

2.3 **Microsleep** — an involuntary sleep episode lasting between 1 and 30 seconds during which the worker is unable to respond to environmental stimuli.

2.4 **Stand-down** — removal of a worker from safety-critical duties pending assessment and authorised return to work under clause 5.

2.5 **Exposure indicator** — a measurement that signals elevated fatigue risk without constituting evidence of a specific causal event.

---

## 3. Biometric Monitoring

3.1 All personnel operating mobile equipment with a gross operating mass exceeding 90 tonnes must wear or use an approved biometric monitoring device throughout their shift.

3.2 A biometric alert is an exposure indicator. It indicates that measured fatigue markers have exceeded the detection threshold and that the worker's risk profile warrants immediate review. A biometric alert is not, on its own, evidence that an incident has occurred, that the worker was impaired at any prior moment, or that the worker caused or contributed to any near-miss event.

3.3 On receipt of a biometric alert, the control-room supervisor must:

a. Contact the worker by radio within 2 minutes.  
b. Instruct the worker to move the equipment to a safe parking position if operationally possible.  
c. Initiate the fatigue-response checklist (OPS-FMS-001-CHK-A) within 5 minutes of the alert.

3.4 Two or more biometric alerts in a single 12-hour shift, or any single alert accompanied by a reported or observed microsleep, require immediate stand-down under clause 5.

---

## 4. Sleep-Deficit Thresholds

4.1 The minimum restorative sleep target is 7 hours in any 24-hour period and 42 hours across any rolling 7-day period.

4.2 A declared or measured sleep deficit of 2 hours or more against the 24-hour target triggers a fitness-for-duty review before the worker commences the next shift.

4.3 A cumulative deficit of 6 hours or more against the 7-day target requires mandatory stand-down from safety-critical duties until the worker records two consecutive qualifying sleep periods (each meeting the 7-hour target).

4.4 Workers are required to declare sleep obtained in the 24 hours before each shift start. Declarations are retained in the shift management system for a minimum of 90 days.

---

## 5. Microsleep Thresholds and Immediate Removal from Task

5.1 Any confirmed or self-reported microsleep during a safety-critical task triggers immediate removal from that task. The worker must stop the task, secure any equipment, and report to the supervisor without delay.

5.2 A worker who records 3 or more microsleep events within a rolling 30-day period must be referred to the site occupational health service for assessment before returning to safety-critical duties, regardless of shift rostering.

5.3 A worker who records 2 or more microsleep events in a single shift must not return to mobile equipment operation or other safety-critical tasks during that shift, irrespective of elapsed time or subjective recovery.

---

## 6. Return to Work Following Stand-Down

6.1 Return to safety-critical duties following a fatigue-related stand-down requires written authorisation from the site Health and Safety Manager or, in their absence, the shift superintendent acting as their delegate.

6.2 Authorisation must be based on at least one of the following:

a. A fitness-for-duty clearance from the site occupational health service.  
b. Documented evidence that the worker has obtained qualifying sleep (meeting the clause 4.1 target) since the stand-down was imposed.  
c. A documented supervisor assessment where (a) and (b) are not achievable within the operational window, with the risk basis recorded and countersigned by the General Manager — Operations or their delegate.

6.3 A copy of the return-to-work authorisation must be lodged in the fatigue management register within 4 hours of the worker resuming duties.

---

## 7. Records and Reporting

7.1 All biometric alerts, stand-downs, microsleep events and return-to-work authorisations must be recorded in the site fatigue management register within 4 hours of the event.

7.2 The Health and Safety Manager must review the fatigue management register at least weekly and submit a monthly trend summary to the General Manager — Operations.

7.3 Any shift on which 3 or more workers receive a biometric alert, or on which 2 or more stand-downs occur, is a reportable fatigue event and must be included in the next scheduled safety performance report to the board-level safety committee.

---

## 8. Contraindications

8.1 This standard does not authorise fatigue risk to be managed solely through administrative controls. Where roster design, travel time or consecutive shift length persistently produce biometric alert rates above 5% of monitored shifts in any 4-week window, the roster must be reviewed by the General Manager — Operations within 14 days.

8.2 No financial incentive, productivity measure or production target may be cited as grounds for waiving a stand-down requirement under clause 5.
