# Grade Reconciliation Standard

**Document reference:** GEO-GRS-003  
**Authorising role:** Chief Geologist  
**Revision:** 4  
**Effective date:** 2024-09-01  
**Review cycle:** 12 months or following any reconciliation variance that breaches the tolerance in clause 3, whichever is sooner  

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

1.1 This standard defines the methodology, variance tolerances, sample-count thresholds, spatial pairing conventions and reporting requirements for grade reconciliation at the operation. It applies to all reconciliation exercises comparing modelled resource-model grade against assayed production grade.

1.2 It applies to the Resource Geology team, the Mine Geology team and any external consultants contracted to perform or audit reconciliation work. It does not apply to geological logging or sampling procedures, which are governed by GEO-SAM-001.

---

## 2. Definitions

2.1 **Modelled grade** — the grade predicted for a spatial block or volume by the current approved resource model, expressed as contained metal per unit mass or volume consistent with the resource model's reporting convention.

2.2 **Assayed grade** — the grade determined from laboratory analysis of samples collected from extracted material, expressed in the same units as the modelled grade for the purposes of comparison.

2.3 **Reconciliation variance** — the percentage difference between modelled grade and assayed grade for a defined spatial domain, calculated as: ((Assayed grade − Modelled grade) / Modelled grade) × 100%.

2.4 **Domain** — a spatially or geologically bounded sub-volume of the deposit used as the unit of analysis in reconciliation reporting, as defined in the current Geological Domains Register.

2.5 **Paired sample** — a sample-to-block pairing in which the sample centroid falls within the search radius defined in clause 5 of the block centroid, and both the sample and the block meet the minimum data-quality criteria in clause 4.3.

2.6 **Indicative reconciliation** — a reconciliation result produced from a paired-sample count below the minimum threshold in clause 4.1, which may not be used for resource model adjustment or production planning without the qualifications in clause 4.2.

---

## 3. Acceptable Variance Tolerance

3.1 The acceptable variance tolerance for grade reconciliation at this operation is ±10% relative on a domain-level basis.

3.2 Domains where the reconciliation variance exceeds ±10% in any quarterly reporting period must be reported to the Chief Geologist and the Mine Planning Manager within 5 business days of the variance being calculated. The report must include the paired-sample count, the search radius applied, and an initial assessment of potential causes.

3.3 Domains where the variance exceeds ±15% relative in two consecutive quarterly periods trigger a mandatory resource model review to be completed within 60 days. The review must assess whether the variance is attributable to model estimation error, sampling error, material movement, or domain boundary misclassification.

3.4 Variances that are within tolerance do not require a root-cause report, but must be recorded in the quarterly reconciliation register.

---

## 4. Minimum Paired-Sample Count

4.1 The minimum number of paired sample-to-block pairs required for a reconciliation result to be reported as representative is 30 pairs per domain per reporting period.

4.2 Where the paired-sample count for a domain falls below 30 pairs, the reconciliation result for that domain must be designated as indicative only in all internal and external reports. An indicative reconciliation result:

a. Must not be used as the sole basis for a resource model adjustment.  
b. Must not be used as the basis for a change to production planning or cut-off grade parameters.  
c. Must carry the notation "Indicative — minimum sample count not met" in all tables and figures in which it appears.

4.3 Samples are excluded from the paired count if they fail any of the following data-quality criteria:

a. Laboratory QA/QC flag indicating a failed duplicate, blank or standard check.  
b. Sample mass below the minimum specified in GEO-SAM-001.  
c. Assay date more than 180 days before the reconciliation reporting date.

4.4 The Mine Geologist is responsible for confirming the paired-sample count before any reconciliation result is reported. The count must be recorded in the reconciliation register alongside the result.

---

## 5. Search Radius Convention for Sample-to-Block Pairing

5.1 The standard search radius for pairing a sample centroid to a block centroid is 15 metres in all three spatial dimensions (isotropic). This radius applies unless a domain-specific radius has been approved under clause 5.3.

5.2 Where a sample centroid falls within the 15-metre radius of more than one block centroid, the sample is assigned to the nearest block by Euclidean distance. If two blocks are equidistant to within 0.1 metres, the sample is assigned to the block with the higher block identifier in the resource model file.

5.3 A domain-specific search radius other than 15 metres may be applied where the geological team has documented evidence that the mineralisation geometry or sample spacing for that domain requires a different radius. Any domain-specific radius must be:

a. Approved in writing by the Chief Geologist before the reconciliation exercise.  
b. Recorded in the Geological Domains Register.  
c. Disclosed in the reconciliation report alongside the rationale.

5.4 The search radius must not be adjusted after the paired-sample count is calculated for the purpose of meeting the minimum threshold in clause 4.1. Any change to the search radius must be applied consistently across all samples in the domain and the rationale documented before the pairing is re-run.

---

## 6. Reporting Requirements

6.1 Reconciliation results must be reported by domain, not in aggregate across the deposit. An aggregate (whole-of-deposit) figure may be included as supplementary information only and must not appear as the headline figure in any report.

6.2 Each reconciliation report must include, for each domain:

a. The reconciliation variance (%), with confidence interval if available.  
b. The number of paired samples used and the number excluded under clause 4.3.  
c. The search radius applied.  
d. Whether the result is designated representative or indicative.  
e. A comparison to the previous two quarterly results to show trend.

6.3 The quarterly reconciliation report must be reviewed and signed by the Chief Geologist and provided to the General Manager — Technical Services within 10 business days of the end of the reporting quarter.

6.4 Where a domain is classified as indicative for three or more consecutive quarters, the Chief Geologist must initiate a targeted infill sampling program or escalate the domain to the resource model review process. The response and its timing must be documented in the reconciliation register.

6.5 All reconciliation data, pairing records and variance calculations must be retained for a minimum of 10 years from the date of the reconciliation report in which they appear.

---

## 7. Constraints

7.1 Grade reconciliation results are point-in-time measurements of the difference between model prediction and production assay. They are not, on their own, a determination of economic loss or gain, and must not be represented as such in communications to external parties without review by the General Manager — Technical Services.

7.2 This standard does not govern reconciliation of tonnage or material classification. Those are addressed in GEO-TRC-004.
